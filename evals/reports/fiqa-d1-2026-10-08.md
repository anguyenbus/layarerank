# Liquid AI d1-3B as a reranker on FiQA — 2026-10-08

Companion to [fiqa-2026-09-28.md](fiqa-2026-09-28.md) (Laya),
[fiqa-decider-2026-10-03.md](fiqa-decider-2026-10-03.md) (Strands Decider) and
[fiqa-kai-2026-10-04.md](fiqa-kai-2026-10-04.md) (Kai). Same pools, same metrics, same gate. The rows
for those systems are their stored per-query scores, not re-runs.

## Summary

- **Off the shelf, d1-3B ties bge and fully fine-tuned Laya.** With no training it scores nDCG@10
  **0.568** on the test split: −0.010 against bge-reranker-v2-m3 [−0.033, +0.012] and −0.012 against
  fully fine-tuned Laya [−0.035, +0.010]. Both intervals include zero.
- **It passes the gate by a wide margin:** +0.176 over BM25 [+0.144, +0.209].
- **It is the best untrained decision model measured here:** +0.050 over untrained Kai [+0.026, +0.073],
  +0.146 over untrained Laya, +0.168 over untrained Strands Decider 2B.
- **It is behind the two fine-tuned decision models:** −0.060 against fine-tuned Strands Decider 2B
  [−0.080, −0.038] and −0.051 against fine-tuned Kai [−0.072, −0.029]. d1-3B was not fine-tuned.
- **Speed:** 25.6 ms per pair on an RTX 3090 (0.77 s for a 30-candidate query). bge re-timed on the
  same card takes 15.1 ms, so d1-3B is about 1.7× slower than bge.
- **Licence:** d1-3B is under the LFM Open License v1.0, which limits commercial use above a revenue
  threshold. bge, Kai and the Decider models are under permissive licences. Check it before adopting.
- **Mapika decider-12b was started and parked.** It ran at about 155 ms per pair and filled the 24 GB
  card; it was stopped after 12 dev queries and has no result (see the last section).
- **Recommendation:** where there is no in-domain training data, d1-3B is now an alternative to bge at
  the same quality. Fine-tuning it with the recipe used for Kai and Decider is the obvious next test.

## Setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Data | BEIR FiQA; BM25 top-30 pools; 385 test and 281 dev answerable queries |
| Pools re-verified | Rebuilt on this machine: BM25 scores 0.3915 nDCG@10 on test and all 385 test and 281 dev candidate lists match the stored ones |
| Metrics | nDCG@10 (primary), MRR@10, Recall@10; paired bootstrap, 5,000 resamples |
| Gate | beats BM25 on nDCG@10 with the 95% CI above 0 |
| Model | `LiquidAI/d1-3B` at commit `051bcc46`; LFM2.5-VL-3B post-trained for decisions, 3.12B parameters, 32,768-token window; bf16, 5.8 GB on the GPU |
| Hardware | one RTX 3090 24 GB; transformers 5.17.0, torch 2.14.0+cu126 |

d1-3B is used pointwise: the state is `{"query", "passage"}`, one question is asked per pair, and the
passages are ranked by the answer. The model ships its runtime as code in the repository, loaded with
`trust_remote_code` from the pinned commit after reading it. The adapter ([evals/d1.py](../d1.py))
calls the runtime's own `probabilities_batch`, which packs a query's 30 pairs into one pass with no
padding and reads the shared query once. Scores are the runtime's unrounded probabilities: P(yes) for a
yes/no question, the expected level for `score`. No temperature or calibration is applied.

Packed scores differ from the one-pair `system_one` call by up to 0.03 (bf16 kernels differ by batch
shape, as the runtime's own comments say); repeated runs of the same query are identical.

## Off-the-shelf presets (dev, 281 queries)

The presets are the ones used for Decider and Kai. BM25 on dev is 0.412.

| Preset | Question | Dev nDCG@10 | Dev MRR@10 | Dev Recall@10 | Δ nDCG vs BM25 on dev |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `noul-criteria` | yes/no with true/false criteria | **0.568** | 0.707 | 0.645 | +0.156 [+0.120, +0.193] |
| `noul-question` | "Does `passage` answer `query`?" | 0.565 | 0.704 | 0.635 | +0.153 [+0.117, +0.191] |
| `score` | 4 levels, "unrelated" … "fully answers", ranked by expected level | 0.549 | 0.687 | 0.617 | +0.137 [+0.099, +0.177] |
| `noul-bare` | "`passage` answers `query`." | 0.548 | 0.679 | 0.631 | +0.136 [+0.100, +0.174] |

`noul-criteria` and `noul-question` were taken to test, where they are tied
(+0.004 [−0.005, +0.013]). Every preset is far above BM25 on dev, and the four are within 0.02 of each
other: d1-3B is much less sensitive to the wording than Laya or Strands Decider were.

## Tied scores

The model's logits are bf16, so the yes/no probability takes few distinct values: on test a query's 30
candidates have about 20 distinct scores with `noul-criteria`, and on 34% of queries ranks 10 and 11
are tied. Ties are broken by BM25 order, as for every system.

This does not move the result. On an 80-query dev sample, running the model in fp32 gives 30 distinct
scores per query and nDCG@10 0.5625, against 0.5651 in bf16, at three times the cost (68 against
21 ms per pair, 13.8 against 6.6 GB). bf16 is kept. The `score` preset has no ties in bf16.

## Caveats

- One dataset. Nothing here says how d1-3B reranks outside FiQA.
- d1-3B was not fine-tuned; the fine-tuned Kai and Decider rows had 3,085 FiQA training queries.
- Latency for Laya, Decider and Kai comes from earlier sessions on other GPUs. Only bge was re-timed
  on the card used here.
- The behaviour analysis done for Laya and Decider (AUC, query groups, thresholds, blends, cascades)
  has not been repeated for d1-3B.
- The probabilities are used as a ranking signal only; their calibration as relevance thresholds was
  not checked.
- The model runs code from its repository. It was read at commit `051bcc46` and the adapter pins that
  commit; a newer commit needs a new read.

## Mapika decider-12b (parked)

[Mapika/decider-12b](https://huggingface.co/Mapika/decider-12b) version 2 (Gemma-4-12B-it with a
merged LoRA, commit `8ac1efa7`) was set up first and stopped at the user's request as too slow. It has
no quality result: 12 dev queries were scored and the file was deleted with the weights.

What was established before it was stopped:

- The bf16 weights take 22.3 GB of the RTX 3090's 23.7 GB. It runs only with the key/value cache
  switched off; peak memory is then about 22.8 GB.
- It takes about 155 ms per pair, 4–5 s per 30-candidate query, the same whether rows are batched or
  not. A dev sweep of four presets would take about 1.5 hours.
- The adapter ([evals/mapika.py](../mapika.py), spec `mapika:12b:<preset>`) reproduces the package's
  own `system_one` answers on the pairs checked. It ranks a yes/no answer by its margin at
  temperature 1, because the checkpoint's serving temperature of 0.05 rounds nearly every answer to 0
  or 1.
- Fine-tuning it does not fit on a 24 GB card.

## Reproduce

```bash
RUN="uv run --extra gpu --extra eval python"

# off the shelf: dev sweep, then the two best on test
for p in noul-question noul-criteria score noul-bare; do
  $RUN -m evals.run --dataset fiqa --k 30 --split validation --system d1:3b:$p
done
$RUN -m evals.run --dataset fiqa --k 30 --system d1:3b:noul-criteria
$RUN -m evals.run --dataset fiqa --k 30 --system d1:3b:noul-question

# table and gate
$RUN -m evals.report --dataset fiqa --k 30 --candidate d1:3b:noul-criteria
```

- The weights (6.2 GB) download from the Hugging Face Hub into `HF_HOME` on first use.
- Per-query scores are in `evals/results/fiqa.top30/` (test) and `evals/results/fiqa-validation.top30/`
  (dev). The dev folder now also holds `bm25.jsonl`.
- The win/tie/loss counts, the bge re-timing and the bf16/fp32 comparison were computed with one-off
  scripts over those files. They are not part of `evals/report.py`.
