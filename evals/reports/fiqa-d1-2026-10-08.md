# Liquid AI d1-3B as a reranker on FiQA — 2026-10-08

Companion to [fiqa-2026-09-28.md](fiqa-2026-09-28.md) (Laya),
[fiqa-decider-2026-10-03.md](fiqa-decider-2026-10-03.md) (Strands Decider) and
[fiqa-kai-2026-10-04.md](fiqa-kai-2026-10-04.md) (Kai). Same pools, same metrics, same gate. The rows
for those systems are their stored per-query scores, not re-runs.

## Summary

- **Off the shelf, d1-3B ties bge and fully fine-tuned Laya.** With no training it scores nDCG@10
  **0.568** on the test split: −0.010 against bge-reranker-v2-m3 [−0.033, +0.012] and −0.012 against
  fully fine-tuned Laya [−0.035, +0.010]. It passes the gate: +0.176 over BM25 [+0.144, +0.209].
- **Fine-tuned, d1-3B ties the two best models measured.** Half an epoch of LoRA training on FiQA
  gives **0.618 nDCG@10**: +0.050 over the untrained model [+0.034, +0.067], +0.040 over bge
  [+0.021, +0.059], −0.000 against fine-tuned Kai [−0.016, +0.016] and −0.009 against fine-tuned
  Strands Decider 2B [−0.024, +0.006].
- **On the top-30 pools, recall@10 is capped by the pool, not the reranker.** BM25's top 30 hold 67%
  of the relevant passages, so no reranker can pass 0.672; the fine-tuned models are all at
  0.644–0.650.
- **A deeper pool plus fine-tuning for recall gives the best recall@10 measured: 0.686.** On BM25's
  top 100 (ceiling 0.750), a d1-3B adapter trained on those pools and kept on dev recall@10 reaches
  **0.686** on the same 385 queries. That is above the 0.672 ceiling of the top-30 pools, +0.045 over
  bge on the same pools [+0.023, +0.068] and +0.067 over untrained d1-3B [+0.045, +0.089]. Its
  nDCG@10 there is 0.636. The deeper pool alone does nothing: untrained d1-3B stays at 0.620 and bge
  moves from 0.633 to 0.642 (§ Recall@10).
- **Speed:** 26 ms per pair untrained and 37 ms with the adapter attached, on an RTX 3090. bge
  re-timed on the same card takes 15 ms.
- **Licence:** d1-3B is under the LFM Open License v1.0, which limits commercial use above a revenue
  threshold. bge, Kai and the Decider models are under permissive licences. Check it before adopting.
- **Mapika decider-12b was started and parked.** It ran at about 155 ms per pair and filled the 24 GB
  card; it was stopped after 12 dev queries and has no result (see the last section).
- **Recommendation:** for recall@10, rerank BM25's top 100 with the recall-tuned d1-3B adapter; it
  costs 100 pairs per query, about 3.6 s on an RTX 3090. Off the shelf, d1-3B is an alternative to bge
  at the same quality. Past this point the first stage is the limit: 25% of the relevant passages are
  not in BM25's top 100.

## Setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Data | BEIR FiQA; BM25 top-30 pools; 385 test and 281 dev answerable queries |
| Pools re-verified | Rebuilt on this machine: BM25 scores 0.3915 nDCG@10 on test and all 385 test and 281 dev candidate lists match the stored ones |
| Metrics | nDCG@10 (primary), MRR@10, Recall@10; paired bootstrap, 5,000 resamples |
| Gate | beats BM25 on nDCG@10 with the 95% CI above 0 |
| Model | `LiquidAI/d1-3B` at commit `051bcc46`; LFM2.5-VL-3B post-trained for decisions, 3.12B parameters, 32,768-token window; bf16, 5.8 GB on the GPU |
| Hardware | one RTX 3090 24 GB; transformers 5.17.0, torch 2.14.0+cu126 |

d1-3B is used pointwise: the state is `{"query", "passage"}`, one question is asked per pair, and the
passages are ranked by the answer. The model ships its runtime as code in the repository, loaded with
`trust_remote_code` from the pinned commit after reading it. The adapter ([evals/d1.py](../d1.py))
calls the runtime's own `probabilities_batch`, which packs a query's 30 pairs into one pass with no
padding and reads the shared query once. Scores are the runtime's unrounded probabilities: P(yes) for a
yes/no question, the expected level for `score`. No temperature or calibration is applied.

Packed scores differ from the one-pair `system_one` call by up to 0.03 (bf16 kernels differ by batch
shape, as the runtime's own comments say); repeated runs of the same query are identical.

## Tied scores

The model's logits are bf16, so the yes/no probability takes few distinct values: on test a query's 30
candidates have about 20 distinct scores with `noul-criteria`, and on 34% of queries ranks 10 and 11
are tied. Ties are broken by BM25 order, as for every system.

This does not move the result. On an 80-query dev sample, running the model in fp32 gives 30 distinct
scores per query and nDCG@10 0.5625, against 0.5651 in bf16, at three times the cost (68 against
21 ms per pair, 13.8 against 6.6 GB). bf16 is kept. The `score` preset has no ties in bf16.

## Fine-tuning

Same recipe as Laya, Decider and Kai ([evals/finetune_decider.py](../finetune_decider.py),
`--family d1`): all positives plus 7 resampled BM25 negatives per query, a listwise softmax over the
yes-minus-no margins plus 0.5 × BCE, 8 queries per step, AdamW with 5% warm-up and cosine decay,
gradient clipping at 1.0, training rows capped at 512 tokens, best checkpoint kept by a dev metric on
a 100-query sample.

- **What trains:** a new rank-16 LoRA adapter (alpha 32) on every linear map of the language model:
  24.5M parameters in fp32. The 3B base stays frozen in bf16. The checkpoint is the adapter alone,
  98 MB.
- **Learning rate:** 1e-4, as for Decider's adapter. Preset: `noul-criteria`.
- **Training pass:** rows run as right-padded chains with gradient checkpointing (peak 10.7 GB);
  scoring keeps the runtime's packed pass. The two agree to within 0.02 in probability.
- **Length:** half an epoch, 193 steps at about 14 s per step: 45 minutes of training, 50 with the
  dev checks.

| Step | Epoch | Dev nDCG@10 (100 queries) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 0.5473 |
| 64 | 0.17 | 0.6108 |
| 128 | 0.33 | 0.6040 |
| **192** | **0.50** | **0.6224** (kept) |
| 193 | 0.50 | 0.6187 |

As with Laya, Decider and Kai, most of the gain arrives in the first sixth of an epoch. The last
three checks are within the noise of a 100-query sample. The run stopped at its cap, not on patience,
so a longer run was not ruled out.

- BM25's top 30 contain 67.2% of the judged-relevant passages, so 0.672 is the most any reranker can
  reach at any K.
- The three fine-tuned decision models are within 0.006 of each other at K = 10 and about 0.025 below
  the ceiling. On recall@10, fine-tuned d1-3B is +0.025 over untrained [+0.012, +0.040], +0.012 over

  [−0.019, +0.012].
- What remains for a reranker is at small K: 0.07 at K = 5 and 0.16 at K = 1.

- **The recall-tuned adapter reaches 0.686 recall@10 on the 385 queries.** That is +0.067 over
  untrained d1-3B on the same pools [+0.045, +0.089], +0.045 over bge [+0.023, +0.068], and above
  every system on the top-30 pools, whose ceiling is 0.672 and whose best is 0.650.
- **The deeper pool only pays off with a model trained for it.** Untrained d1-3B has the same
  recall@10 on 100 candidates as on 30 (0.620 against 0.619) and a lower nDCG@10 (0.549 against
  0.568): the 70 extra candidates add as many distractors as answers. bge gains 0.009.
- **Ranking quality rises with it.** nDCG@10 is 0.636 on the 385 queries, against 0.618 for the same
  model family on the top-30 pools. Kai and Decider were not scored on the top-100 pools, so this is
  not a comparison with them.
- **The same adapter on the top-30 pools matches the first one:** 0.616 nDCG@10 and 0.644 recall@10,
  against 0.618 and 0.644. Selecting on recall@10 instead of nDCG@10 changes nothing there; the gain
  comes from the deeper pool.
- **Headroom left:** 0.064 to the top-100 ceiling at K = 10. Beyond that the first stage is the limit:
  BM25's top 100 hold 75% of the relevant passages for these queries.
- **Cost:** 100 pairs per query at 36 ms, about 3.6 s per query on the RTX 3090, against 1.1 s for 30.
- What was not separated: the gain could come from the deeper training pools, the selection metric,
  or both. The adapter kept on nDCG@10 was not scored on the top-100 pools.

## Caveats

- One dataset. Nothing here says how d1-3B reranks outside FiQA, fine-tuned or not.
- One training run per adapter: one seed, one learning rate, half an epoch or less. Both runs ended at
  their step cap.
- The checkpoints were chosen on a 100-query dev sample, and the fine-tuned adapters were not scored
  on the full dev split.
- The fine-tune differs from Decider's and Kai's in what trains and for how long, so the ties are a
  comparison of results, not a controlled comparison of the models.
- bge was not fine-tuned.
- On the top-100 pools only BM25, bge and d1-3B were scored. Laya, Kai and Decider have no top-100
  rows, and the adapter kept on nDCG@10 was not scored there.
- Latency for Laya, Decider and Kai comes from earlier sessions on other GPUs. Only bge was re-timed
  on the card used here. The untrained d1-3B top-100 run shared the GPU with training, so its timing
  is not quoted.
- The behaviour analysis done for Laya and Decider (AUC, query groups, thresholds, blends, cascades)
  has not been repeated for d1-3B.
- The probabilities are used as a ranking signal only; their calibration as relevance thresholds was
  not checked.
- The model runs code from its repository. It was read at commit `051bcc46` and the adapter pins that
  commit; a newer commit needs a new read.
- The adapters `evals/models/d1-ft-fiqa/` and `evals/models/d1-ft-fiqa-r10/` (98 MB each) are
  git-ignored and sit on container storage that does not survive a recycle or destroy.

## Mapika decider-12b (parked)

[Mapika/decider-12b](https://huggingface.co/Mapika/decider-12b) version 2 (Gemma-4-12B-it with a
merged LoRA, commit `8ac1efa7`) was set up first and stopped at the user's request as too slow. It has
no quality result: 12 dev queries were scored and the file was deleted with the weights.

What was established before it was stopped:

- The bf16 weights take 22.3 GB of the RTX 3090's 23.7 GB. It runs only with the key/value cache
  switched off; peak memory is then about 22.8 GB.
- It takes about 155 ms per pair, 4–5 s per 30-candidate query, the same whether rows are batched or
  not. A dev sweep of four presets would take about 1.5 hours.
- The adapter ([evals/mapika.py](../mapika.py), spec `mapika:12b:<preset>`) reproduces the package's
  own `system_one` answers on the pairs checked. It ranks a yes/no answer by its margin at
  temperature 1, because the checkpoint's serving temperature of 0.05 rounds nearly every answer to 0
  or 1.
- Fine-tuning it does not fit on a 24 GB card.

## Reproduce

```bash
RUN="uv run --extra gpu --extra eval python"

# off the shelf: dev sweep, then the two best on test
for p in noul-question noul-criteria score noul-bare; do
  $RUN -m evals.run --dataset fiqa --k 30 --split validation --system d1:3b:$p
done
$RUN -m evals.run --dataset fiqa --k 30 --system d1:3b:noul-criteria
$RUN -m evals.run --dataset fiqa --k 30 --system d1:3b:noul-question

# fine-tune on the top-30 pools (kept on nDCG@10), then score
PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True $RUN -m evals.finetune_decider --family d1 \
  --preset noul-criteria --out d1-ft-fiqa --epochs 0.5 --evals-per-epoch 6 --dev-queries 100 \
  --patience 3 --micro-queries 2
$RUN -m evals.run --dataset fiqa --k 30 --system d1:d1-ft-fiqa:noul-criteria

# fine-tune on the top-100 pools (kept on recall@10), then score both pool depths
PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True $RUN -m evals.finetune_decider --family d1 --k 100 \
  --select recall@10 --preset noul-criteria --out d1-ft-fiqa-r10 --epochs 0.4 --evals-per-epoch 8 \
  --dev-queries 100 --patience 3 --micro-queries 2
for s in bm25 bge d1:3b:noul-criteria d1:d1-ft-fiqa-r10:noul-criteria; do
  $RUN -m evals.run --dataset fiqa --k 100 --system $s
done
$RUN -m evals.run --dataset fiqa --k 30 --system d1:d1-ft-fiqa-r10:noul-criteria

# table and gate
$RUN -m evals.report --dataset fiqa --k 30 --candidate d1:d1-ft-fiqa:noul-criteria
```

- The weights (6.2 GB) download from the Hugging Face Hub into `HF_HOME` on first use.
- Per-query scores are in `evals/results/fiqa.top30/` and `evals/results/fiqa.top100/` (test) and
  `evals/results/fiqa-validation.top30/` (dev). The dev folder now also holds `bm25.jsonl`.
- The dev curves and run settings are in `finetune.json` beside each adapter (not in git).
- The recall@K tables, win/tie/loss counts, the bge re-timing and the bf16/fp32 comparison were
  computed with one-off scripts over those files. They are not part of `evals/report.py`.
